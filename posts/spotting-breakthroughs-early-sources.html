Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Boudreau 2016 Boudreau, Guinan, Lakhani & Riedl Looking Across and Looking Beyond the Knowledge Frontier Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2285 RCT (2,130 evaluator-proposal pairs): evaluators score highly novel proposals lower — anchors "we flinch from the breakthroughs."
DellaVigna 2018 DellaVigna & Pope Predicting Experimental Results: Who Knows What? J. Political Economy (topecon) 10.1086/699976 Average forecast beats 96% of individuals; expertise markers don't predict accuracy — anchors "crowds, not experts."
Salganik 2006 Salganik, Dodds & Watts Inequality and Unpredictability in an Artificial Cultural Market Science (web) 10.1126/science.1121066 Music-lab experiment: social influence raises inequality AND unpredictability — the hook and the luck/path-dependence leg.
Sinatra 2016 Sinatra, Wang, Deville, Song & Barabási Quantifying the evolution of individual scientific impact Science (web) 10.1126/science.aaf5239 Random-impact rule + Q-model (ability stable, luck random) — the "talent is real, timing is luck" nuance.
Ke 2015 Ke, Ferrara, Radicchi & Flammini Defining and identifying Sleeping Beauties in science PNAS (web) 10.1073/pnas.1424329112 Beauty coefficient; delayed recognition on a continuum; EPR/Pearson examples — the "sleeping beauties" section.
Wang-Veugelers 2017 Wang, Veugelers & Stephan Bias against novelty in science Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2017.06.006 Novel papers: more top-1% hits long-run but delayed, higher-variance, in lower-IF journals — noisy/biased early signal.
Azoulay 2011 Azoulay, Graff Zivin & Manso Incentives and creativity: evidence from the academic life sciences RAND J. Economics (fieldindustrialorganization) 10.1111/j.1756-2171.2011.00140.x HHMI vs NIH (PSM + DiD): failure-tolerant funding → more high-impact work AND more flops — the portfolio/tolerate-failure case.
Gyorffy 2020 Győrffy, Herman & Szabó Research funding: past performance is a stronger predictor of future output than reviewer scores J. Informetrics (fieldbibliometrics) 10.1016/j.joi.2020.101050 Reviewer scores have weak predictive validity vs a simple track record — the faint-signal point + lottery rationale.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Wang-Song 2013 Wang, Song & Barabási Quantifying Long-Term Scientific Impact Science (web) 10.1126/science.1237825 The prominent claim that impact IS predictable (3-parameter universal curve) — stated, then critiqued.
Wang-Mei 2014 Wang, Mei & Hicks Comment on "Quantifying long-term scientific impact" Science (web) 10.1126/science.1248770 The "formal critique in the same journal": predictions frequently far off, often worse than early counts.
Penner 2013 Penner, Pan, Petersen, Kaski & Fortunato On the Predictability of Future Impact in Science Scientific Reports (web) 10.1038/srep03052 Apparent predictability is largely an autocorrelation/career-age artifact; worst for the young.
Uzzi 2013 Uzzi, Mukherjee, Stringer & Jones Atypical Combinations and Scientific Impact Science (web) 10.1126/science.1240474 Conventional core + novel tail ≈ 2× more likely highly cited — a real ex-ante structural signal (honest counter).
Camerer 2018 Camerer, Dreber, Holzmeister et al. Evaluating the replicability of social science experiments Nature Human Behaviour (web) 10.1038/s41562-018-0399-z Prediction markets forecast replication well (rank corr ≈ 0.84) — experts collectively CAN predict (counter).
Camerer 2016 Camerer, Dreber, Forsell et al. Evaluating replicability of laboratory experiments in economics Science (web) 10.1126/science.aaf0918 The earlier econ version (n=18): markets positive but NOT significant — the honest limit on "markets predict."
Lane 2022 Lane, Teplitskiy, Gray et al. Conservatism Gets Funded? Negative Information in Novel Project Evaluation Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2021.4107 Evaluators weight negative information more for novel projects — bias mechanism.
Chai 2019 Chai & Menon Breakthrough recognition: Bias against novelty and competition for attention Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2018.11.006 5.3M papers: rarely-addressed topics more likely to break through; corroborates bias against the conventional/novel.
Tian 2014 Tian & Wang Tolerance for Failure and Corporate Innovation Review of Financial Studies (fieldfinance) 10.1093/rfs/hhr130 Failure-tolerant VCs → significantly more innovative startups (private-sector mirror of Azoulay).
Heyard 2022 Heyard, Ott, Salanti & Egger Rethinking the Funding Line at the SNSF: Bayesian Ranking and Lottery Statistics and Public Policy (web) 10.1080/2330443X.2022.2086190 Inter-rater reliability is low near the funding line → rationale for tie-break lotteries.
