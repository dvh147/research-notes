Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
DMM2017 Dechezleprêtre, Martin & Mohnen (2014, rev. 2017) Knowledge spillovers from clean and dirty technologies Grantham/CEP WP (web) https://eprints.lse.ac.uk/60501/ Headline: clean patents +43% citations vs dirty; comparable to IT; radical-novelty mechanism; clean spillovers carry more market value.
MV2023 Martin & Verhoeven (2023) Knowledge spillovers from clean innovation: A tradeoff between growth and climate? CEP DP1933 (web) https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/121306/ The "compared to what?" benchmark: clean returns 1/8 higher than an untargeted R&D dollar; no growth-climate tradeoff; cross-border leakage; coordination gains (25% EU, >60% global); IRA.
AABH2012 Acemoglu, Aghion, Bursztyn & Hémous (2012) The Environment and Directed Technical Change AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.102.1.131 Theory: two market failures → two instruments (research subsidies + carbon taxes) redirect innovation toward clean; delay is costly.
ADHMV2016 Aghion, Dechezleprêtre, Hémous, Martin & Van Reenen (2016) Carbon Taxes, Path Dependency, and Directed Technical Change: Auto Industry JPE (topecon) 10.1086/684581 Direct evidence the direction of innovation responds to prices and to spillovers; clean begets clean (path dependence from aggregate spillovers + own history).
ML2022 Myers & Lanahan (2022) Estimating Spillovers from Publicly Funded R&D: Evidence from the US DOE AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20210678 Energy-R&D spillovers are large (≈3 spillover patents per grant patent) and 60% stay within the US (magnitude + leakage).
AAHK2016 Acemoglu, Akcigit, Hanley & Kerr (2016) Transition to Clean Technology JPE (topecon) 10.1086/684511 Quantitative optimal-policy model on US energy microdata: optimal path uses both subsidies and carbon taxes; carbon-tax-only / delay is costly.

Takalo2013 Takalo, Tanayama & Toivanen (2013) Estimating the Benefits of Targeted R&D Subsidies REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00280 Targeted R&D subsidies can carry social returns of 30–50% (order-of-magnitude for back-of-envelope).
JS2024 Jee & Srivastav (2024) Knowledge spillovers between clean and dirty technologies: Evidence from the patent citation network Ecological Economics 224 (web/field) 10.1016/j.ecolecon.2024.108310 Objection/nuance: clean and dirty are mostly indirectly linked in the citation network; low direct spillovers ↔ stronger path dependence.
AABH2023 Acemoglu, Aghion, Barrage & Hémous (2023) Green innovation and the transition toward a clean economy PIIE WP 23-14 (web) https://www.piie.com/publications/working-papers/2023/green-innovation-and-transition-toward-clean-economy Modern directed-technical-change synthesis; redirecting innovation toward green without sacrificing growth.

Background / seminal lineage

Key Authors (year) Title Source DOI / URL Why included
JTH1993 Jaffe, Trajtenberg & Henderson (1993) Geographic Localization of Knowledge Spillovers as Evidenced by Patent Citations QJE (topecon) 10.2307/2118401 Founding use of patent citations as a spillover proxy, and the original evidence that spillovers are geographically localized (the method behind the clean/dirty result; the lineage of "spillovers leak across borders").
AG2006 Alcácer & Gittelman (2006) Patent citations as a measure of knowledge flows: The influence of examiner citations REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/rest.88.4.774 The measurement caveat: ~two-thirds of citations on the average patent are examiner-inserted, adding noise to citation-based spillover inference (the "noisy ruler" objection).
