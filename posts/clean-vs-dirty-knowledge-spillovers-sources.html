Core papers (read in full and summarized)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
DMM2022 Dechezleprêtre, Martin & Mohnen (2022) Knowledge Spillovers from Clean and Dirty Technologies Working paper, version of 29 May 2022 (web) https://drive.google.com/file/d/1uHkXEWfqkqwdoIi_EHnC8tGNWQBVyQy3/view The clean-versus-dirty comparison: about 1.4 million inventions in electricity production and transport, 1950–2012, clean vs dirty from the same patent office and year with quality controls. Authors' summary: "up to 60% higher levels of spillovers"; text: 60–68% more citations; main table coefficient 0.631 (about 88% more when converted exactly — our arithmetic); text and tables do not always agree. Robust to dropping examiner and self-citations and to inventor fixed effects; premium has grown since the early 1980s (Figure 4); roughly halves when technology age is controlled for (Table 11); not clearly different from IT, AI, wireless, biotech, nanotech, robots or 3D printing, clearly ahead of aerospace (Table 12); dirty energy below the average invention; no difference in average private value of clean and dirty patents (Table 14); the authors say targeted support "could equally be justified" for AI, nanotech or IT. Earlier versions: CEP DP 1300, 2014 (43%) and Grantham WP 135, Oct 2017 update ("up to 40%").
MV2023 Martin & Verhoeven (2023) Knowledge spillovers from clean innovation: A tradeoff between growth and climate? CEP DP1933 (web) https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/121306/ Clean versus the average research dollar: each patent's spillover value is credited back from the value of later inventions that cite it, directly or through chains; return to an extra subsidy dollar computed by field. Clean 135% worldwide vs 120% for size-proportional support (one-eighth higher; second of six fields; electrical engineering about 168%). About 23% of clean spillover value stays in the country of origin (24% for all innovation; US about 32%). On domestic returns clean is about 30 cents per dollar vs 28 on average; its advantage grows in the EU, shrinks in Japan and China, vanishes in the US. Coordination gains about 25% EU, 40% OECD/G7, over 60% global (China takes about 84% of the globally optimal subsidy). EU benefits from US clean spillovers (IRA). No dirty category in this paper.
BMR2020 Barbieri, Marzucchi & Rizzo (2020) Knowledge sources and impacts on subsequent inventions: Do green technologies differ from non-green ones? Research Policy 49(2) 103901 (web/field) 10.1016/j.respol.2019.103901 Independent comparison of green vs non-green: about 1.07 million patent families with European and US filings, 1980–2012, compared within the same 4-digit technology class; green families get 31.8% more citations within 5 years (Table 4 col. 5), about 20% after complexity and novelty controls (Table 5 col. 5), and are cited from a wider spread of fields. Read as SEEDS WP 08/2019.
BCDU2026 Buzzacchi, Croce, De Marco & Ughetto (2026) Knowledge spillovers from green technologies Technovation 103526 (web/field) 10.1016/j.technovation.2026.103526 Independent comparison of green vs non-green: 3.8 million European Patent Office applications, 1981–2020, 8.3% climate-tagged, compared with non-green applications of the same year and field: about 24% more citations (Table 14), about 22% counting indirect citations (Table 15), slightly more citing fields; cited by inventors about 12% farther away, within-country share 3.6 points lower (Table 18; citations by inventors in selected EU states). Raw means show almost no gap; premium peaked in 2001–10.
AABH2012 Acemoglu, Aghion, Bursztyn & Hémous (2012) The Environment and Directed Technical Change AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.102.1.131 Theory: when clean and dirty inputs are sufficiently substitutable, temporary taxes/subsidies redirect innovation to clean for good (Prop. 3); optimal policy uses a carbon tax and a research subsidy (Prop. 5), a tax alone is costly (Table 2). Clean and dirty research are modelled alike, so the subsidy redirects research away from carbon rather than rewarding a clean spillover premium.
ADHMV2016 Aghion, Dechezleprêtre, Hémous, Martin & Van Reenen (2016) Carbon Taxes, Path Dependency, and Directed Technical Change: Auto Industry JPE (topecon) 10.1086/684581 About 3,400 car-industry patent holders, 1986–2005; each holder's fuel price is a mix of 25 countries' prices weighted by where it patented in 1965–85: a 10% higher fuel price goes with about 10% more clean and 6% fewer dirty patents (Table 3); clean patenting associated with own past clean patents and other firms' clean knowledge (path dependence, associations).
ML2022 Myers & Lanahan (2022) Estimating Spillovers from Publicly Funded R&D: Evidence from the US DOE AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20210678 DOE small-business grants, variation from state top-ups of federal grants, spillovers detected without citations: about three more patents by others per grant-recipient patent; foreign patentees produce 41% of all extra patents (Table 3) — the abstract's "60% of spillovers within the US" counts recipients' own patents, and among spillover patents alone about 46% are US (our arithmetic); many in distant technologies; citation links would miss about half of the spillovers (p. 2415); grants span all twelve DOE offices including fossil and nuclear, so not a clean-vs-dirty comparison.
AAHK2016 Acemoglu, Akcigit, Hanley & Kerr (2016) Transition to Clean Technology JPE (topecon) 10.1086/684511 Model of the US energy sector estimated on 1,576 firms' data (1975–2004): optimal policy pairs a large early clean-research subsidy with a carbon tax; carbon tax alone costs 1.9% of consumption; without carbon damages the planner would not subsidize clean research (read as HBS WP 15-045, 2014).

BSV2013 Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen (2013) Identifying Technology Spillovers and Product Market Rivalry Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA9466 US firms 1980–2001, closeness in technology space vs product-market space, tax-credit variation: social returns to R&D at least twice private (about 55% vs 21%).
LRR2026 Lupi, Rentocchini & Rizzo (2026) Forever Young? On the technological impact of green startups Technological Forecasting and Social Change 124840 (web/field) 10.1016/j.techfore.2026.124840 US patenting firms 1976–2019: green start-ups' inventions get about 20% (3 years) and 8% (7 years) more citations than other start-ups' (Table 1); premium concentrated in green technologies and fades as firms mature.
PN2012 Popp & Newell (2012) Where does energy R&D come from? Examining crowding out from energy R&D Energy Economics 34(4) 980–991 (web/field) 10.1016/j.eneco.2011.07.001 Alternative-energy patents cited more often and by more technologies than the same firms' other patents; within firms, more alternative-energy patents mean fewer other patents; no crowding out across industries.
EMRTV2026 Edet, Martin, de Rochambeau, Tran & Verhoeven (2026) Innovation partnerships for development along green value chains CEP DP2223 (web) https://cep.lse.ac.uk/pubs/download/dp2223.pdf Developing-economy inventors: marginal return to green R&D about 93% vs 61% overall (global spillovers); green premium about 1 percentage point for spillovers within developing economies (p. 7).
JS2024 Jee & Srivastav (2024) Knowledge spillovers between clean and dirty technologies: Evidence from the patent citation network Ecological Economics 224 (web/field) 10.1016/j.ecolecon.2024.108310 Fewer than one in ten clean patents cite dirty directly; nearly two-thirds linked indirectly; bridging fields; low direct spillovers mean stronger path dependence.
Popp2002 Popp (2002) Induced innovation and energy prices AER (topecon) 10.1257/000282802760015658 US patents 1970–1994: higher energy prices induce energy-efficient innovation.
DEMNV2023 Dechezleprêtre, Einiö, Martin, Nguyen & Van Reenen (2023) Do Tax Incentives Increase Firm Innovation? An RD Design for R&D, Patents, and Spillovers AEJ: Economic Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20200739 UK firms around a size threshold for R&D tax relief: relief raises patenting and spills over to technologically close firms.
AHSV2021 Akcigit, Hanley & Serrano-Velarde (2021) Back to Basics: Basic Research Spillovers, Innovation Policy, and Growth REStud (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdaa061 Model estimated on French firms: uniform research subsidies oversubsidize applied research.
Takalo2013 Takalo, Tanayama & Toivanen (2013) Estimating the Benefits of Targeted R&D Subsidies REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00280 Finnish project-level data: social rate of return on targeted subsidies 30–50%.
Karydas2017 Karydas (2017) The inter-temporal dimension to knowledge spillovers: any non-environmental reason to support clean innovation? CER-ETH Economics WP 17/267 (web) https://ideas.repec.org/p/eth/wpswif/17-267.html Theory against: with diffusion lags, wide applicability of green innovation alone does not justify higher subsidies.
BHP2025 Barbieri, Hötte & Persoon (2025) The evolving boundary of green technology arXiv 2503.21310 (web) https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21310 Measurement: reclassification and wider coverage add about 9% and 10% green patents; newly visible ones more novel and cited.
SRZ2025 Santarlasci, Rungi & Zinilli (2025) Seeing Through Green: Text-Based Classification and the Firm's Returns from Green Patents arXiv 2507.02287 (web) https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.02287 Measurement: only about 18.5% of code-classified green patents are green by their text.
