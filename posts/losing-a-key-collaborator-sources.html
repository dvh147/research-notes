Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Azoulay-GraffZivin-Wang 2010 Azoulay, P.; Graff Zivin, J. S.; Wang, J. Superstar Extinction QJE (topecon) 10.1162/qjec.2010.125.2.549 The foundational result: a superstar's premature death cuts collaborators' quality-adjusted output 5-8%, lastingly; loss is "circumscribed in idea space."
Jaravel-Petkova-Bell 2018 Jaravel, X.; Petkova, N.; Bell, A. Team-Specific Capital and Innovation AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20151184 Replicates in patents: co-inventor death -> -15% citation-weighted patents, -4% earnings at 8 yrs; rules out firm/network/top-down; mechanism = "experience" in close-knit teams.
Waldinger 2012 Waldinger, F. Peer Effects in Science: Evidence from the Dismissal of Scientists in Nazi Germany REStud (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdr029 The disciplining null: losing high-quality local peers (non-collaborators) has no effect -> it's the collaboration, not proximity, that carries productivity.
Azoulay-FonsRosen-GraffZivin 2019 Azoulay, P.; Fons-Rosen, C.; Graff Zivin, J. S. Does Science Advance One Funeral at a Time? AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20161574 The objection/twist: after a star's death, non-collaborators' article flow rises ~8.6% and is more highly cited -> field gains even as collaborators lose.
Oettl 2012 Oettl, A. Reconceptualizing Stars: Scientist Helpfulness and Peer Performance Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.1110.1470 Mechanism: coauthors of helpful dead stars lose 14-16% in output quality; unhelpful stars' deaths do nothing; conceptual feedback matters most -> knowledge complementarity, not access.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Chen 2021 Chen, Y. Team-Specific Human Capital and Team Performance: Evidence from Doctors AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20201238 Team-specific capital generalizes and is life-or-death: +1 SD shared work experience -> 10-14% lower 30-day patient mortality.
Mohnen 2022 Mohnen, M. Stars and Brokers: Knowledge Spillovers Among Medical Scientists Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2021.4032 Network position matters: +1 SD in a deceased star's brokerage -> ~10% drop in coauthors' annual publications (loss of access to non-redundant knowledge).
Bartel et al. 2014 Bartel, A. P., et al. Human Capital and Productivity in a Team Environment: Evidence from the Healthcare Sector AEJ: Applied (subtopecon) 10.1257/app.6.2.231 Generalizes beyond research: departures of experienced nurses (and churn) lower a hospital unit's productivity beyond skill/experience levels.
Moretti 2021 Moretti, E. The Effect of High-Tech Clusters on the Productivity of Top Inventors AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20191277 Honest caveat: agglomeration does raise inventor productivity — but consistent with idea-space (clusters supply collaborators), not bare proximity.

Background / seminal lineage

Key Authors (year) Title Source DOI / URL Why included
Wuchty-Jones-Uzzi 2007 Wuchty, S.; Jones, B. F.; Uzzi, B. The Increasing Dominance of Teams in Production of Knowledge Science (web) 10.1126/science.1136099 Stakes/framing: team-authored share of social-science papers rose 17.5% (1955) -> 51.5% (2000); teams produce the most-cited work.
Waldinger 2010 Waldinger, F. Quality Matters: The Expulsion of Professors and the Consequences for PhD Student Outcomes in Nazi Germany JPE (web; not in corpus) 10.1086/655976 The vertical tie: losing high-quality faculty badly hurts students (+13 pp top-journal dissertation per SD faculty quality) — mentoring carries capital too.
Borjas-Doran 2012 Borjas, G. J.; Doran, K. B. The Collapse of the Soviet Union and the Productivity of American Mathematicians QJE (web; not in corpus) 10.1093/qje/qjs015 The mirror image: gaining competitors in your idea-space lowers incumbent output — unifies with the "one funeral" result.
