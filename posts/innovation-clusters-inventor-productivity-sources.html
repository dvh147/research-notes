Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Moretti 2021 Moretti, E. The Effect of High-Tech Clusters on the Productivity of Top Inventors AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20191277 The headline inventor-level causal claim: mover design + IV; elasticity 0.067, +12% (median→75th cluster), ~11% aggregate.
Wiebe 2026 Wiebe, M. The Effect of High-Tech Clusters on the Productivity of Top Inventors: Comment AER (topecon) · web 10.1257/aer.20231415 The contestation: event study + IV both go null when corrected; "may not be causal." Earns the title's "(probably)".
GHM 2010 Greenstone, M.; Hornbeck, R.; Moretti, E. Identifying Agglomeration Spillovers: Evidence from Winners and Losers of Large Plant Openings JPE (topecon) 10.1086/653714 Clean-ID agglomeration spillover: +12% incumbent TFP in winning vs runner-up counties; channels = labor pooling + tech proximity.
Combes et al. 2012 Combes, P.-P.; Duranton, G.; Gobillon, L.; Puga, D.; Roux, S. The Productivity Advantages of Large Cities: Distinguishing Agglomeration From Firm Selection Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA8442 Answers the selection objection: selection explains ~none; agglomeration elasticity ~0.03; +9.7% (dilation +14.4% top vs +4.8% bottom).
de la Roca & Puga 2017 de la Roca, J.; Puga, D. Learning by Working in Big Cities REStud (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdw031 The dynamic ("learning") channel; half the premium is dynamic, portable (14.5% retained), larger for abler workers; sorting ruled out.
Kerr & Kominers 2015 Kerr, W. R.; Kominers, S. D. Agglomerative Forces and Cluster Shapes REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00471 Microfoundation: spillover reach sets cluster shape; knowledge premium halves within 15-20 mi; high-tech clusters densest.
Glaeser & Gottlieb 2009 Glaeser, E. L.; Gottlieb, J. D. The Wealth of Cities: Agglomeration Economies and Spatial Equilibrium in the United States JEL (subtopecon) 10.1257/jel.47.4.983 The price side: spatial equilibrium capitalizes productivity into wages + rents; income elasticity ~0.04/doubling.
Diamond 2016 Diamond, R. The Determinants and Welfare Implications of US Workers' Diverging Location Choices by Skill: 1980-2000 AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20131706 The distributional sting: well-being inequality rose ~30% more than the wage gap once endogenous amenities counted.
Kline & Moretti 2014 Kline, P.; Moretti, E. Local Economic Development, Agglomeration Economies, and the Big Push: 100 Years of Evidence from the TVA QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjt034 Policy: a big push seeds a durable cluster, but is ~zero-sum nationally; national gain came from infrastructure.

Zacchia 2018 Zacchia, P. Benefiting colleagues but not the city: Localized effects from the relocation of superstar inventors Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2018.03.004 Spillovers run through networks (direct collaborators), not ambient geography.
Azoulay et al. 2010 Azoulay, P.; Graff Zivin, J.; Wang, J. Superstar Extinction QJE (topecon) 10.1162/qjec.2010.125.2.549 Value lives in specific ties: collaborators' output falls after a superstar's death.
