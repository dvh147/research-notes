Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Feigenbaum-Gross 2024 Feigenbaum, J.; Gross, D. Answering the Call of Automation QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjae005 Anchor for the whole claim: one episode showing aggregate adjustment (future cohorts fine) AND concentrated incumbent loss.
Edin-et-al 2023 Edin, P-A.; Evans, T.; Graetz, G.; Hernnäs, S.; Michaels, G. Individual Consequences of Occupational Decline Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/uead027 Generalizes the incidence half: workers in declining occupations lose 2-5% of career earnings on average, but up to 8-11% for those at the bottom.
Acemoglu-Restrepo 2020 Acemoglu, D.; Restrepo, P. Robots and Jobs: Evidence from US Labor Markets JPE (topecon) 10.1086/705716 The objection: where adjustment is weak, automation lowers aggregate local employment (-0.2pp emp-pop, -0.42% wages per robot/1,000 workers).
Dauth-et-al 2021 Dauth, W.; Findeisen, S.; Suedekum, J.; Woessner, N. The Adjustment of Labor Markets to Robots JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvab012 The institutional contrast: German robot displacement fully offset by service jobs; incidence falls on young entrants, incumbents protected.
Autor-et-al 2024 Autor, D.; Chin, C.; Salomons, A.; Seegmiller, B. New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940-2018 QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjae008 The reinstatement engine: majority of current employment is in new job specialties since 1940; but automation-erosion has intensified in recent decades.
Braxton-Taska 2023 Braxton, J. C.; Taska, B. Technological Change and the Consequences of Job Loss? AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20210182 The bridge between halves: tech change explains 45% of post-job-loss earnings decline by devaluing incumbents' skills.
Sullivan-vonWachter 2009 Sullivan, D.; von Wachter, T. Job Displacement and Mortality QJE (topecon) 10.1162/qjec.2009.124.3.1265 Depth of the incumbent cost: displacement raises mortality 50-100% the year after, 10-15% even 20 years later; ~1-1.5 years of life expectancy.
Jacobson-LaLonde-Sullivan 1993 Jacobson, L.; LaLonde, R.; Sullivan, D. Earnings Losses of Displaced Workers AER (topecon) https://www.jstor.org/stable/2117574 Canonical magnitude of the incumbent cost: high-tenure displaced workers lose ~25%/year, persistently.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Graetz-Michaels 2018 Graetz, G.; Michaels, G. Robots at Work REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/rest_a_00754 Robots raised productivity (~0.36pp/yr) and did not significantly cut total employment, though they cut the low-skill employment share.
AutorDornHanson 2015 Autor, D.; Dorn, D.; Hanson, G. Untangling Trade and Technology Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1111/ecoj.12245 Technology/automation causes polarization but no net local employment decline; trade (China) causes net decline — the key distinction.
Mann-Puttmann 2023 Mann, K.; Püttmann, L. Benign Effects of Automation: New Evidence from Patent Texts REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/rest_a_01083 Automation patents raise local employment on net, via service-sector job growth.
Gregory-Salomons-Zierahn 2022 Gregory, T.; Salomons, A.; Zierahn, U. Racing With or Against the Machine? JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvab040 Routine-replacing tech displaced labor in Europe 1999-2010 but net created jobs via product-demand channel.
Autor-Dorn 2013 Autor, D.; Dorn, D. The Growth of Low-Skill Service Jobs and the Polarization of the US Labor Market AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.103.5.1553 Where displaced routine workers go: reallocation into low-skill service occupations.
ALM 2003 Autor, D.; Levy, F.; Murnane, R. The Skill Content of Recent Technological Change QJE (topecon) 10.1162/003355303322552801 The task framework: machines substitute for routine tasks, complement non-routine ones.
AcemogluRestrepo 2022 Acemoglu, D.; Restrepo, P. Tasks, Automation, and the Rise in US Wage Inequality Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA19815 Task displacement explains 50-70% of changes in the US wage structure since 1980 — the distributional bite.
AcemogluRestrepo 2018 Acemoglu, D.; Restrepo, P. The Race between Man and Machine AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20160696 Theory of automation vs new-task creation; the stabilizing force that encourages new work.
Lin 2011 Lin, J. Technological Adaptation, Cities, and New Work REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00079 New work concentrates in dense, educated cities — adaptation is local.
Battisti-et-al 2023 Battisti, M.; Dustmann, C.; Schoenberg, U. Technological and Organizational Change and the Careers of Workers JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvad014 Firms retrain routine workers into abstract jobs (no earnings/employment penalty) — except older workers, who exit. Incidence again concentrated.
Couch-Placzek 2010 Couch, K.; Placzek, D. Earnings Losses of Displaced Workers Revisited AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.100.1.572 Replication of JLS with cleaner data: persistent losses (~13-15% six years out).
Lachowska-Mas-Woodbury 2020 Lachowska, M.; Mas, A.; Woodbury, S. Sources of Displaced Workers' Long-Term Earnings Losses AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20180652 Losses driven by wage-rate (not just hours) declines; loss of specific match > lost firm premium.
Schmieder-vonWachter-Heining 2023 Schmieder, J.; von Wachter, T.; Heining, J. The Costs of Job Displacement over the Business Cycle and Its Sources AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20200252 Displacement losses large, persistent, and ~double in downturns — context/timing matter.
Bonfiglioli-et-al 2024 Bonfiglioli, A.; Crinò, R.; Fadinger, H.; Gancia, G. Robot Imports and Firm-Level Outcomes Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/ueae055 Exogenous robot exposure causes firm-level job losses (naive correlation is positive) — identification matters.
Autor-Thompson 2025 Autor, D.; Thompson, N. The Expertise JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvaf023 Resolves why routine automation cut employment yet raised wages in some occupations: it depends on whether removed tasks were expert or inexpert.
