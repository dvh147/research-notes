Sources — "Radical," "disruptive," and "breakthrough" are not the same thing

Every source cited or consulted for this post. Roles: seed (drew the topic), core (analyzed in depth, anchors an argument), supporting (cited for a specific point), background (seminal lineage / context). Corpus papers carry their Web-of-Science category; items verified on the open web are marked accordingly. No claim in the post draws on a source that is not listed here, and no citation is fabricated.

Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
Verhoeven, Kovács, Marullo, Di Minin & Van Looy (2025) More or the same? … Industrial and Corporate Change (innovation field) 10.1093/icc/dtae045 The spine: 4,407 articles + 60 seminal definitions show the labels are non-distinctive and collapse onto two dimensions, novelty and impact.
Verhoeven, Bakker & Veugelers (2016) Measuring technological novelty with patent-based indicators Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2015.11.010 Novelty decomposes into Recombination vs Knowledge-Origins; novelty raises the variance/tail of impact, not the mean — separating novelty from impact.
Kaplan & Vakili (2015) The double-edged sword of recombination in breakthrough innovation Strategic Management Journal (top management) 10.1002/smj.2294 Cognitive novelty (local search) and economic value (broad recombination) have different antecedents — novelty ≠ impact.
Yang (2025) Text vs. citations: A comparative analysis of breakthrough and disruption metrics in patent innovation Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2025.105295 Head-to-head of the KI (text breakthrough) and CD (citation disruption) indices on 6M+ patents: correlated but diverge on time-trend, impact relation, and team size.
Govindarajan & Kopalle (2006) Disruptiveness of innovations: Measurement and an assessment of reliability and validity Strategic Management Journal (top management) 10.1002/smj.511 Survey-scale evidence (199 SBUs, 38 Fortune 500) that disruptiveness is a construct distinct from radicalness.
Keijl, Gilsing, Knoben & Duysters (2016) The two faces of inventions: recombination and impact in pharmaceutical biotechnology Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2016.02.008 Inverted-U: intermediate recombination, not the most novel, carries the highest impact.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
Jung & Lee (2016) The quest for originality: a new typology of knowledge search and breakthrough inventions Academy of Management Journal (top management) 10.5465/amj.2014.0756 An "originality" axis of search; local search of original knowledge yields high-impact breakthroughs — another distinct novelty dimension.
Arts & Veugelers (2015) Technology familiarity, recombinant novelty, and breakthrough invention Industrial and Corporate Change (innovation field) 10.1093/icc/dtu029 New combinations of familiar components raise breakthroughs and lower failure — novelty has internal structure with different risk profiles.
Schoenmakers & Duysters (2010) The technological origins of radical inventions Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2010.05.013 Radical inventions are built more on existing knowledge, recombined across more domains — "radical ≠ from scratch."
Strumsky & Lobo (2015) Identifying the sources of technological novelty in the process of invention Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2015.05.008 Four distinct sources of novelty; plain reuse recently overtook recombination — a taxonomy of novelty types.
Nemet & Johnson (2012) Do important inventions benefit from knowledge originating in other technological domains? Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2011.08.009 Counter-evidence: distant/external prior art predicts lower impact than close prior art — novelty and impact can point opposite ways.
Veugelers & Wang (2019) Scientific novelty and technological impact Research Policy (innovation field) 10.1016/j.respol.2019.01.019 Scientific-origin novelty (top 1% novel science) has broader technological impact — the novelty→impact link is type-specific.
Datta & Srivastava (2023) (Re)conceptualizing technological breakthrough innovation: a systematic review Technological Forecasting and Social Change (innovation field) 10.1016/j.techfore.2023.122740 Independent systematic review (86 articles) corroborating that "breakthrough" is used synonymously with radical/disruptive/discontinuous.

Background / seminal lineage

Authors (year) Title Source DOI / URL Why included
Christensen, Raynor & McDonald (2015) What Is Disruptive Innovation? Harvard Business Review 93(12):44–53 hbr.org The hook: the term's originators argue "disruptive" is used far too broadly (Uber isn't disruptive by the strict definition). Web-verified.
Christensen (1997) The Innovator's Dilemma Harvard Business School Press (book) — Origin of the "disruptive innovation" concept. Referenced for lineage; no DOI.
Henderson & Clark (1990) Architectural Innovation: The Reconfiguration of Existing Product Technologies and the Failure of Established Firms Administrative Science Quarterly 35(1):9–30 10.2307/2393549 The classic 2×2 (incremental/modular/architectural/radical) — the field already knew "radical vs incremental" was too coarse. Web-verified.
Dahlin & Behrens (2005) When is an invention really radical? Defining and measuring technological radicalness Research Policy 34(5):717–737 10.1016/j.respol.2005.03.009 Defines "radical" via three criteria (novel + unique + impactful) — literally bundling novelty and impact into one word. Web-verified.
Funk & Owen-Smith (2017) A Dynamic Network Measure of Technological Change Management Science 63(3):791–817 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2366 Origin of the CD (consolidate/destabilize) disruption index used in Yang's comparison. Web-verified.

Notes on verification

  • The 2×2 table and the tail-overlap back-of-the-envelope in the post are the author's own illustrations of the argument; they are not numerical results reported in any cited paper, and are flagged as such in the text.
  • Specific magnitudes from corpus papers are taken from abstracts (directions of effects); where a precise effect size could not be independently verified, the post reports the direction only.
