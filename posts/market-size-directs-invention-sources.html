Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
Acemoglu-Linn 2004 Acemoglu & Linn Market Size in Innovation QJE (topecon) 10.1162/0033553041502144 Headline causal elasticity: 1% market size -> 4-6% more new drugs (exogenous demographics).
Finkelstein 2004 Finkelstein Health Policy & the Vaccine Industry QJE 10.1162/0033553041382166 Engineered demand (3 vaccine policies) -> 2.5x trials; $1 revenue -> 5-6c R&D.
Blume-Kohout-Sood 2013 Blume-Kohout & Sood Effects of Medicare Part D on pharmaceutical R&D J. Public Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2012.10.003 Part D -> +31-33% preclinical, +18-38% clinical entries for elderly-used classes.
Zhang-Nie 2021 Zhang & Nie Public health insurance and pharmaceutical innovation: China J. Development Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2020.102578 China rural insurance -> +12.4% patents for newly-covered diseases; states the thesis.
Geng-Shi 2024 Geng & Shi Health policy, price regulation, and innovation: China's vaccines J. Development Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2023.103229 More volume + price controls -> -83% trials: it's revenue, not patients.
Dubois et al. 2015 Dubois, de Mouzon, Scott-Morton & Seabright Market size and pharmaceutical innovation RAND J. Economics (fieldindustrialorganization) 10.1111/1756-2171.12113 Calibration: aggregate elasticity ~0.23; ~$2.5bn revenue per new chemical entity.
Kyle-McGahan 2012 Kyle & McGahan Investments in Pharmaceuticals Before and After TRIPS Rev. Economics & Statistics (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00214 TRIPS patents raised R&D in rich countries, NOT for developing-country diseases.
Moscona-Sastry 2023 Moscona & Sastry Does Directed Innovation Mitigate Climate Damage? US Agriculture QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjac039 Generality + distributional caveat: ag innovation offset 20% of US damage; ignored non-US distress.
Hanlon 2015 Hanlon Necessity Is the Mother of Invention: Input Supplies and Directed Technical Change Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA10811 Historical natural experiment: Civil War cotton shock redirected British textile invention.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
Costinot et al. 2019 Costinot, Donaldson, Kyle & Williams The More We Die, The More We Sell? (home-market effect) QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjz003 Demand pulls direction across borders: countries net-export the drugs they most demand.
Duggan et al. 2016 Duggan, Garthwaite & Goyal Market Impacts of Pharmaceutical Product Patents in Developing Countries: India American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20141301 India 2005 patents raised prices only 3-6%: IP not binding where market is poor.
Goldberg 2010 Goldberg IPR Protection in Developing Countries: Pharmaceuticals (Marshall Lecture) JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1111/j.1542-4774.2010.tb00506.x In poor countries the binding effect of patents is lost access, not price.
Goldberg-Reed 2023 Goldberg & Reed Demand-Side Constraints in Development (Presidential Address) Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA20787 Macro echo: small/poor domestic markets too small to support advanced technology.
Lin 1992 Lin Hybrid Rice Innovation in China: Market-Demand Induced Innovation Rev. Economics & Statistics (subtopecon) 10.2307/2109537 Generality: provincial rice acreage (market size) drove rice research.
Lakdawalla 2018 Lakdawalla Economics of the Pharmaceutical Industry J. Economic Literature (subtopecon) 10.1257/jel.20161327 Background survey of pharma innovation/pricing economics.

Background / seminal lineage

Key Authors (year) Title Source DOI / URL Why included
Schmookler 1966 Schmookler Invention and Economic Growth Harvard Univ. Press (web) (book; no DOI) Origin of the demand-pull thesis (invention follows sales/investment).
Mowery-Rosenberg 1979 Mowery & Rosenberg The influence of market demand upon innovation: a critical review Research Policy (web) 10.1016/0048-7333(79)90019-2 The classic critique (need vs demand; tautology) that exogenous-demand designs answer.
Kremer 2002 Kremer Pharmaceuticals and the Developing World J. Economic Perspectives (web) 10.1257/089533002320950984 The market-failure framing for neglected diseases (poor markets -> little R&D).
