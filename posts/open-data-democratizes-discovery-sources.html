Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
Nagaraj 2022 Nagaraj, A. The Private Impact of Public Data (Landsat & gold) Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2020.3878 The anchor: open Landsat data nearly doubled gold discoveries AND raised entrants' share from ~10% to ~25% (cloud-cover/timing ID). Both halves of the claim in one design.
Nagaraj, Shears & de Vaan 2020 Nagaraj, A.; Shears, E.; de Vaan, M. Improving data access democratizes and diversifies science PNAS (web) 10.1073/pnas.2001682117 Academic counterpart: cheaper Landsat access raised quantity+quality of science, gains disproportionately to developing-world and lower-ranked institutions (~24k papers).
Biasi & Moser 2021 Biasi, B.; Moser, P. Effects of Copyrights on Science (WWII Book Republication Program) AEJ: Microeconomics (subtopecon) 10.1257/mic.20190113 Cheaper literature: ~25% price drop; each 10% price fall -> ~43% more citations; follow-on driven by less-affluent institutions. Different input/era, same democratization signature.
Howell, Rathje, Van Reenen & Wong 2025 Howell, S.; Rathje, J.; Van Reenen, J.; Wong, J. Opening Up Military Innovation Journal of Political Economy (topecon) 10.1086/737235 Open vs conventional Air Force R&D (simultaneous arms): open awards +12pp VC, +9pp patents, +11pp military adoption; conventional did nothing but lock-in; open reached younger non-incumbent firms; effect survives beyond selection.
Iaria, Schwarz & Waldinger 2018 Iaria, A.; Schwarz, C.; Waldinger, F. Frontier Knowledge and Scientific Production (collapse of international science) Quarterly Journal of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjy011 Mechanism in reverse: losing access to frontier knowledge after 1914 cut output most for the most-dependent (top-1%-reliant fell ~2x top-3%). Access drives production.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
Galasso & Schankerman 2015 Galasso, A.; Schankerman, M. Patents and Cumulative Innovation: Causal Evidence from the Courts Quarterly Journal of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qju029 Court invalidation -> +50% follow-on, driven entirely by small firms building on large patentees. Convergent outsider-tilt from the IP side.
Watzinger, Fackler, Nagler & Schnitzer 2020 Watzinger, M.; Fackler, T.; Nagler, M.; Schnitzer, M. How Antitrust Enforcement Can Spur Innovation: Bell Labs and the 1956 Consent Decree AEJ: Economic Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20190086 Forced royalty-free licensing -> lasting follow-on rise, mostly young/small firms (and only outside telecom). Convergent outsider-tilt; the product-market caveat.
Murray, Aghion, Dewatripont, Kolev & Stern 2016 Murray, F.; Aghion, P.; Dewatripont, M.; Kolev, J.; Stern, S. Of Mice and Academics: Examining the Effect of Openness on Innovation AEJ: Economic Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20140062 Opening access to research mice -> more DIVERSE follow-on and new researchers entering, no drop in new-strain creation. Diversity/entry leg; also the create-the-input reassurance.
