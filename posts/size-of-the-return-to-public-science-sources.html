Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Jaffe 1989 Jaffe Real Effects of Academic Research AER (topecon) JSTOR 1831431 Early correlational anchor: elasticity of corporate patenting w.r.t. university research ~0.2 (larger for "innovation"); drugs & electronics; the kind of correlation selection should bias up.
Mansfield 1991/98 Mansfield Academic research and industrial innovation (+ 1998 update) Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/0048-7333(91)90080-A · 1998: 10.1016/S0048-7333(97)00043-7 The canonical "old large number": ~11% products / ~9% processes depend on recent academic research (drugs ~27-29%); ~28% average social rate of return (conservative ~10%); ~7yr lag.
Adams 1990 Adams Fundamental Stocks of Knowledge and Productivity Growth JPE (topecon) 10.1086/261702 Productivity-accounting channel: academic knowledge a major contributor to manufacturing TFP, with very long lags (~20yr overall, ~30yr for the most fundamental science).
Azoulay 2019 Azoulay, Graff Zivin, Li & Sampat Public R&D Investments and Private-sector Patenting (NIH funding rules) Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdy034 Modern natural experiment: $10M NIH -> ~2.3-2.7 private-sector patents; $1 -> $0.70-$2.13 lifetime drug sales; effect at firms, not grantees.
Myers-Lanahan 2022 Myers & Lanahan Estimating Spillovers from Publicly Funded R&D (US DOE) American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20210678 The spillover magnitude: ~3 spillover patents per recipient patent; recipients capture only 25-50% of value; the appropriation-share toy example.
Gross-Sampat 2023 Gross & Sampat America, Jump-Started: WWII R&D and the Takeoff of the US Innovation System American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20221365 Historical natural experiment: doubling OSRD share -> ~20% more patents by 1960, ~30% by 1970, persisting for decades; "permanently changed the trajectory of US innovation."
Cohen-Nelson-Walsh 2002 Cohen, Nelson & Walsh Links and Impacts: The Influence of Public Research on Industrial R&D Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.48.1.1.14273 Mechanism (Carnegie Mellon Survey): public research feeds industrial R&D pervasively, mainly via OPEN channels (papers > conferences > informal > consulting >> patents) -> citation trails undercount.
Fleming 2019 Fleming, Greene, Li, Marx & Yao Government-funded research increasingly fuels innovation Science 10.1126/science.aaw2373 Scale/prevalence: ~1/3 of US patents rely on federally funded research (chemistry ~60%); risen since the 1970s; such patents more cited and renewed.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Toole 2012 Toole The impact of public basic research on industrial innovation (pharma) Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2011.06.004 The pharma lineage: NIH basic research -> new-drug entry once long lags are allowed for; fits the convergence pattern.
Griliches 1992 Griliches The Search for R&D Spillovers Scand. J. Economics 94(Suppl):S29-S47 NBER w3768 The social-vs-private wedge: "social rates of return remain significantly above private rates" (gap ~50-100% of private).
Bloom-Schankerman-Van Reenen 2013 Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen Identifying Technology Spillovers and Product Market Rivalry Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA9466 Gross social returns to R&D >= 2x private returns.
Zacchia 2020 Zacchia Knowledge Spillovers through Networks of Scientists Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdz033 Marginal social return to R&D ~112% of private, via scientist networks.
Salter-Martin 2001 Salter & Martin The economic benefits of publicly funded basic research: a critical review Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/S0048-7333(00)00091-3 The honesty anchor: six benefit channels; "no simple model of the economic benefits from basic research is possible" — a single ROI is the wrong target.
Babina 2023 Babina, He, Howell, Perlman & Staudt Cutting the Innovation Engine Quarterly Journal of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjac046 The composition warning: funding cuts can RAISE (low-quality, privately assigned) patenting while cutting basic publications/startups — raw patent counts mislead.

Verification notes (provenance & confidence)

  • Read directly / high confidence: Mansfield 1991 figures (PDF + CBO 1993 review): 11%/9% dependence, 28% average social return, 7yr lag. Adams lag structure (20/~30yr, from the abstract). Cohen-Nelson-Walsh channel ranking and dual-role finding (abstract + companion paper). Fleming ~1/3 and chemistry ~60% (multiple consistent sources). Salter-Martin six channels and "no simple model" caution. DOIs for Adams, CNW, Fleming, Salter-Martin, Mansfield, Gross-Sampat confirmed resolving.
  • Solid but secondary-sourced (medium confidence), flagged in prose: Gross-Sampat magnitudes (~20%/30%) — from the AER paper's abstract plus NBER Digest / AEA / ITIF summaries, not a direct table read. Fleming's "tripled since the 1970s" trend. Griliches' "50-100% of private" gap (NRC 2000 paraphrase of his survey judgment; his verified core statement is qualitative).
  • Deliberately NOT cited as a number: Adams's specific TFP-contribution percentages (could not be traced to a citable source) — the post claims only "a major contributor" with long lags.
  • Jaffe elasticity (~0.2/0.4): widely repeated in the secondary literature; the exact table value was not re-read from the paywalled original, so the prose says "on the order of 0.2."
  • Per-paper working notes with full provenance are in working/. Modern-anchor notes (Azoulay, Myers-Lanahan, Toole) were carried over from the 2026-06-07 run and were web-verified there.
