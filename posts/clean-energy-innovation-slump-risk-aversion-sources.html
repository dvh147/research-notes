Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Gaddy et al. 2017 Gaddy, B.; Sivaram, V.; Jones, T.; Wayman, L. (2017) Venture Capital and Cleantech: The wrong model for energy innovation Energy Policy (web) https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301421516306991 The cleantech bust: >$25bn invested 2006–11, >half lost; worst returns in capital-intensive "deep tech". Anchors the hook + "money problem" shape.
KPSS 2017 Kogan, L.; Papanikolaou, D.; Seru, A.; Stoffman, N. (2017) Technological Innovation, Resource Allocation, and Growth QJE (topecon) https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjw040 Stock-market measure of a patent's private value; tracks citations & predicts firm growth. Establishes that private value can be measured (the seed paper's lineage).
Nanda & Rhodes-Kropf 2013 Nanda, R.; Rhodes-Kropf, M. (2013) Investment cycles and startup innovation JFE (fieldfinance) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2013.07.001 Hot capital markets fund riskier, more innovative startups; cold markets retreat to safe bets. The core financing-cycle mechanism.
Ewens et al. 2018 Ewens, M.; Nanda, R.; Rhodes-Kropf, M. (2018) Cost of experimentation and the evolution of venture capital JFE (fieldfinance) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.03.001 Capital flows toward cheap/fast-to-test innovation and away from complex tech where experiments cost more and reveal less — a description of clean energy. The hinge paper.
Howell 2017 Howell, S. T. (2017) Financing Innovation: Evidence from R&D Grants AER (topecon) https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20150808 DOE early-stage energy grants ~double subsequent VC, strongest for constrained firms, by funding prototyping (de-risking). The policy-lever evidence.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
IEA/EPO 2021 International Energy Agency & European Patent Office (2021) Patents and the Energy Transition IEA/EPO report (web) https://www.iea.org/reports/patents-and-the-energy-transition Low-carbon energy patents falling since 2012; 2017–19 growth +3.3%/yr vs +12.5%/yr in 2000–13; part of the slowdown is technology maturity.
Kerr & Nanda 2009 Kerr, W. R.; Nanda, R. (2009) Democratizing entry: Banking deregulations, financing constraints, and entrepreneurship JFE (fieldfinance) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2008.12.003 Creative destruction needs many failures; winners hard to identify ex ante → need to bankroll a wide, lossy portfolio.
Cochrane 2005 Cochrane, J. H. (2005) The risk and return of venture capital JFE (fieldfinance) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfineco.2004.03.006 VC returns are violently skewed (fat right tail) → tolerance for variance is the binding input.
Doblinger et al. 2019 Doblinger, C.; Surana, K.; Anadon, L. D. (2019) Governments as partners: The role of alliances in US cleantech startup innovation Research Policy (fieldinnov) https://doi.org/10.1016/j.respol.2019.02.006 657 US cleantech startups 2008–12: +1 govt tech alliance ≈ +74% patenting; a govt license ≈ +155% private financing deals. Public risk-absorption crowds in capital (correlational).
Acemoglu et al. 2012 Acemoglu, D.; Aghion, P.; Bursztyn, L.; Hémous, D. (2012) The Environment and Directed Technical Change AER (topecon) https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.102.1.131 Optimal climate policy uses both a carbon tax and research subsidies; delay is costly.
Acemoglu et al. 2016 Acemoglu, D.; Akcigit, U.; Hanley, D.; Kerr, W. (2016) Transition to Clean Technology JPE (topecon) https://doi.org/10.1086/684511 Estimated energy-sector model: optimal path relies heavily on both subsidies and taxes; carbon-tax-only or delay carries large welfare costs.
