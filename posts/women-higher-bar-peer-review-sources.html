Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Hengel 2022 Hengel Publishing While Female Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/ueac032 The readability fingerprint: women's papers 1–6% more readable, gap widens in review, women improve over career; ~6 months longer under review.
Card et al. 2020 Card, DellaVigna, Funk, Iriberri Are Referees and Editors in Economics Gender Neutral? QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjz035 The bar at the journal: referee gender neutral, but +25% citations conditional on referee score; editors 1.7pp below citation-max R&R.
Hospido & Sanz 2021 Hospido, Sanz Gender Gaps in the Evaluation of Research: Evidence from Submissions to Economics Conferences Oxford Bull. Econ. & Stats (web) 10.1111/obes.12409 Third econ design: all-female papers −3.3pp (6.8%) acceptance; driven by male referees, lesser-known authors, finance.
Squazzoni et al. 2021 Squazzoni, Bravo, Grimaldo, et al. Peer review and gender bias: A study on 145 scholarly journals Science Advances (web) 10.1126/sciadv.abd0299 The key calibration/objection: across 145 journals peer review is NOT anti-women — so the claim is field-specific to econ.
Ellison 2002 Ellison Evolving Standards for Academic Publishing: A q–r Theory JPE (topecon) 10.1086/341871 The mechanism + toy model: a rising r-threshold applied to women yields longer review and cleaner published papers.
Botelho & Abraham 2017 Botelho, Abraham Pursuing Quality: How Search Costs and Uncertainty Magnify Gender-based Double Standards Admin. Sci. Quarterly (topman) 10.1177/0001839217694358 Why a higher bar: double standards bite hardest under uncertainty/high search costs (matches the lesser-known-author pattern).

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Mengel et al. 2019 Mengel, Sauermann, Zölitz Gender Bias in Teaching Evaluations JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvx057 The evaluative lens on women is biased (random assignment; equal grades, lower evals).
Eberhardt et al. 2023 Eberhardt, Facchini, Rueda Gender Differences in Reference Letters: Evidence from the Economics Job Market Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/uead045 Women described as "grindstone," less for ability — a different yardstick in econ.
Horbach et al. 2022 Horbach, Schneider, Sainte-Marie Ungendered Writing: Writing Styles Are Unlikely to Account for Gender Differences in Funding Rates J. Informetrics (fieldbibliometrics) 10.1016/j.joi.2022.101332 Raw writing-style gender differences are minimal → the published readability gap is process-made.
Blank 1991 Blank The Effects of Double-Blind versus Single-Blind Reviewing: Experimental Evidence from the AER American Economic Review (topecon; no DOI) RePEc/IDEAS Double-blind makes referees tougher but not differentially for women → blinding is a weak lever.
Pleskac et al. 2025 Pleskac, Kyung, Chapman, Urminsky Blinded versus Unblinded Review: A Field Study on the Equity of Peer-Review Processes Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2022.01646 Modern field study: double-blind is no cure-all; if anything men edge up under blinding.
Bohnet et al. 2016 Bohnet, van Geen, Bazerman When Performance Trumps Gender Bias: Joint vs. Separate Evaluation Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2186 The most promising fix: joint evaluation makes evaluators weight performance over stereotype.
