Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
adhvaryu-2023 Adhvaryu, Kala & Nyshadham (2023) Returns to On-the-Job Soft Skills Training JPE (topecon) 10.1086/724320 The seed; central RCT: P.A.C.E. soft-skills training -> +13.5% productivity, ~256% firm return, no wage rise, coworker spillover.
heckman-2006 Heckman, Stixrud & Urzua (2006) The Effects of Cognitive and Noncognitive Abilities on Labor Market Outcomes and Social Behavior J. Labor Economics (fieldothers) 10.1086/504455 Foundation: the noncognitive trait moves wages/schooling/behavior comparably to cognitive ability.
deming-2017 Deming (2017) The Growing Importance of Social Skills in the Labor Market QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjx022 Demand side: social-skill jobs +12pp of the labor force 1980-2012; social and cognitive skills are complements.
weidmann-deming-2021 Weidmann & Deming (2021) Team Players: How Social Skills Improve Team Performance Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA18461 Causal proof that social skill is a real, transferable input (+0.13 SD to team output), predicted by social intelligence not IQ.
degrip-2012 De Grip & Sauermann (2012) The Effects of Training on Own and Co-worker Productivity Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2012.02500.x RCT: 1-week communication course -> +~10% performance; spillover of +0.51% per 10pp trained peers.
campos-2017 Campos et al. (2017) Teaching personal initiative beats traditional training in boosting small business in West Africa Science (web) 10.1126/science.aan5329 RCT: personal-initiative (soft) training +~30% profits vs +11% (n.s.) for business-content training.
masmoretti-2009 Mas & Moretti (2009) Peers at Work AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.99.1.112 Mechanism: productivity is contagious (+1.5% per 10% coworker productivity) via observation/social pressure.
card-2018 Card, Kluve & Weber (2018) What Works? A Meta-Analysis of Recent Active Labor Market Program Evaluations JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvx028 Counter-evidence/calibration: avg training effect ~0 short-run, grows medium-run; bigger for the disadvantaged.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
lindqvist-vestman-2011 Lindqvist & Vestman (2011) The Labor Market Returns to Cognitive and Noncognitive Ability: Evidence from the Swedish Enlistment AEJ: Applied (subtopecon) 10.1257/app.3.1.101 Noncognitive skill protects against the bottom (unemployment, low earnings); cognitive matters more at the top.
bloom-2013 Bloom, Eifert, Mahajan, McKenzie & Roberts (2013) Does Management Matter? Evidence from India QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjs044 Management is a teachable skill: +17% productivity (but intensive, expensive consulting).
gosnell-2020 Gosnell, List & Metcalfe (2020) The Impact of Management Practices on Employee Productivity: A Field Experiment with Airline Captains JPE (topecon) 10.1086/705375 Cheap management practices (monitoring, targets) raise even skilled professionals' productivity.
iacovone-2022 Iacovone, Maloney & McKenzie (2022) Improving Management with Individual and Group-Based Consulting: ... Colombia Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdab005 Group delivery at ~1/3 cost works as well -> the approach can be scaled cheaply.
cornelissen-2017 Cornelissen, Dustmann & Schonberg (2017) Peer Effects in the Workplace AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20141300 Peer effects are larger in low-skill, observable jobs, small in high-skill ones -> bounds the spillover claim.
card-2010 Card, Kluve & Weber (2010) Active Labour Market Policy Evaluations: A Meta-Analysis Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2010.02387.x Earlier meta-analysis (199 estimates); consulted, corroborates the 2018 pattern.
mckenzie-woodruff-2014 McKenzie & Woodruff (2014) What Are We Learning from Business Training and Entrepreneurship Evaluations...? World Bank Research Observer (web) 10.1093/wbro/lkt007 Generic business training changes practices but profit effects are often modest/short-lived.
demel-2014 de Mel, McKenzie & Woodruff (2014) Business training and female enterprise start-up, growth, and dynamics: ... Sri Lanka J. Development Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2013.09.005 Training alone did nothing for existing owners' profits; capital (a grant) was the durable mover.
resnjanskij-2024 Resnjanskij, Ruhose, Wiederhold, Woessmann & Wedel (2024) Can Mentoring Alleviate Family Disadvantage in Adolescence? JPE (topecon) 10.1086/726905 Soft intervention (mentoring) raises low-SES youth outcomes >0.5 SD; nothing for high-SES -> heterogeneity/targeting.
konings-vanormelingen-2015 Konings & Vanormelingen (2015) The Impact of Training on Productivity and Wages: Firm-Level Evidence Review of Economics and Statistics (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00460 Trained worker's productivity premium ~23% vs wage premium ~12% -> firm captures the wedge.
acemoglu-pischke-1999 Acemoglu & Pischke (1999) The Structure of Wages and Investment in General Training JPE (topecon) 10.1086/250071 Frictions/wage compression let firms fund and capture returns to general training.
acemoglu-pischke-1998 Acemoglu & Pischke (1998) Why Do Firms Train? Theory and Evidence QJE (topecon) 10.1162/003355398555531 Companion to the 1999 theory; firms train in general skills under labor-market frictions.
