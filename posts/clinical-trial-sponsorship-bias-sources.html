Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Oostrom 2024 Oostrom, T. Funding of Clinical Trials and Reported Drug Efficacy JPE (topecon) 10.1086/730383 The central causal estimate: same-drug-pair design; ~50% sponsorship effect; publication bias is the main channel; Effexor/Prozac case.
Lundh 2017 Lundh, Lexchin, Mintzes, Schroll, Bero Industry sponsorship and research outcome Cochrane Database Syst Rev (web) 10.1002/14651858.MR000033.pub3 External validity: meta-analysis across drugs+devices; RR 1.27 efficacy, 1.34 conclusions; bias not explained by risk-of-bias.
Turner 2008 Turner, Matthews, Linardatos, Tell, Rosenthal Selective Publication of Antidepressant Trials and Its Influence on Apparent Efficacy NEJM (web) 10.1056/NEJMsa065779 The mechanism: 74 FDA-registered trials, 51% positive vs 94% in print; effect-size inflation 11–69% (32% class).
Kaplan & Irvin 2015 Kaplan, R.M.; Irvin, V.L. Likelihood of Null Effects of Large NHLBI Clinical Trials Has Increased over Time PLoS ONE (web) 10.1371/journal.pone.0132382 The remedy: positive-result rate fell 57%→8% after the 2000 registration requirement.
Salandra 2018 Salandra, R. Knowledge dissemination in clinical trials: ... selective reporting Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2018.04.005 Localizes the lever: selective reporting higher for industry-funded studies, but only with an industry-affiliated author.
Ioannidis et al. 2017 Ioannidis, Stanley, Doucouliagos The Power of Bias in Economics Research Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1111/ecoj.12461 Cross-domain anchor: median power ~18%, ~80% of effects exaggerated (typically 2×).
Andrews & Kasy 2019 Andrews, I.; Kasy, M. Identification of and Correction for Publication Bias AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20180310 Formal grounding that selective publication distorts estimates and is correctable.
Chopra et al. 2023 Chopra, Haaland, Roth, Stegmann The Null Result Penalty Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/uead060 Demand-side cause: nulls penalized at fixed quality; motivates pre-result review.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Lexchin 2003 Lexchin, Bero, Djulbegovic, Clark Pharmaceutical industry sponsorship and research outcome and quality BMJ (web) 10.1136/bmj.326.7400.1167 OR 4.05 for pro-sponsor outcomes; industry trials not lower quality → comparator + publication bias.
Kao 2025 Kao, J. Information Disclosure and Competitive Dynamics: Evidence from the Pharmaceutical Industry Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2022.01161 Strategic non-disclosure: a rival's approval cuts reporting of own trial by 13%.
Brodeur et al. 2023 Brodeur, Carrell, Figlio, Lusher Unpacking p-Hacking and Publication Bias AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20210795 Bunching of test statistics at significance thresholds.
Young 2019 Young, A. Channeling Fisher: Randomization Tests and the Statistical Insignificance of Seemingly Significant Experimental Results QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjy029 13–22% fewer significant individual effects (a third–half fewer jointly) under randomization inference.
Fanelli 2012 Fanelli, D. Negative results are disappearing from most disciplines and countries Scientometrics (fieldbibliometrics) 10.1007/s11192-011-0494-7 Positive-support rate rose ~22% (1990–2007).
Fanelli 2013 Fanelli, D. Positive results receive more citations, but only in some disciplines Scientometrics (fieldbibliometrics) 10.1007/s11192-012-0757-y Positive results cited ~32% more in applied fields like clinical medicine.
Askarov et al. 2023 Askarov, Doucouliagos, Doucouliagos, Stanley The Significance of Data-Sharing Policy JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvac053 Data-sharing mandates reduced reported statistical significance (footprint of less selective reporting).
Henry 2009 Henry, E. Strategic Disclosure of Research Results: The Cost of Proving Your Honesty Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1111/j.1468-0297.2009.02265.x Theory: binding disclosure forces a biased sender to reveal everything.
Carey et al. 2021 Carey, Lieber, Miller Drug firms' payments and physicians' prescribing behavior in Medicare Part D J. Public Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2021.104402 Calibrating non-result: payments shift prescribing, but drug quality is unaffected — bias is about the picture, not a hidden truth.
Brodeur et al. 2024 Brodeur, Cook, Neisser p-Hacking, Data type and Data-Sharing Policy Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/uead104 Data-sharing alone does not reduce p-hacking — remedies are partial.
Naguib 2025 Naguib, C. P-hacking and Significance Stars Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/ueaf073 Removing significance stars (2016) had no detectable effect — cosmetic reforms don't bite.
