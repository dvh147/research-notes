Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Budish 2015 Budish, Roin & Williams Do Firms Underinvest in Long-Term Research? American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20131176 Centerpiece: the fixed patent clock distorts cancer R&D toward short trials; magnitudes (trials by stage; +10pp survival -> -8.7% R&D; surrogate endpoints +112%; ~890k life-years / ~$89B).
Moser 2005 Moser How Do Patent Laws Influence Innovation? American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/0002828054825501 Frame: patents shape the direction of innovation, not the amount; most 1851 exhibits unpatented; secrecy substitutes.
Galasso 2015 Galasso & Schankerman Patents and Cumulative Innovation: Causal Evidence from the Courts Quarterly Journal of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qju029 Patent invalidation (random judges) -> +50% follow-on, but only in computers/electronics/medical instruments, not drugs/chemicals; large patentees block small firms.
Sampat 2019 Sampat & Williams How Do Patents Affect Follow-On Innovation? Evidence from the Human Genome American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20151398 Honest counter-evidence: gene patents have no quantitatively important average effect on follow-on; documents selection into patenting.
MoserVoena 2012 Moser & Voena Compulsory Licensing: Evidence from the Trading with the Enemy Act American Economic Review (topecon) 10.1257/aer.102.1.396 Forcing patents open raised US domestic invention ~20% (chemicals); strong patents can suppress domestic follow-on.
Murray 2016 Murray, Aghion, Dewatripont, Kolev & Stern Of Mice and Academics: Examining the Effect of Openness on Innovation AEJ: Economic Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20140062 Lower IP-style access costs -> more entry and more diverse research; "a neglected cost of strong IP: less exploration."

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL Point it supports
Williams 2013 Williams Intellectual Property Rights and Innovation: Evidence from the Human Genome Journal of Political Economy (web) 10.1086/669706 Celera's contractual gene IP cut follow-on research/products 20-30% — the contrast that motivates Sampat & Williams (2019).
Freilich 2023 Freilich & Shahshahani Measuring follow-on innovation Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2023.104854 Reconciles the gene-patent results: 87% of follow-on doesn't infringe; chilling effect only for patents far from expiration.
Sakakibara 2001 Sakakibara & Branstetter Do Stronger Patents Induce More Innovation? Evidence from the 1988 Japanese Patent Law Reforms RAND J. of Economics (web) NBER w7066 Broadening Japanese patent scope produced no detectable rise in R&D or innovation (307 firms).
Lerner 2002 Lerner 150 Years of Patent Protection American Economic Review P&P (web) NBER w8977 Across 60 countries and 177 reforms, strengthening patents had few positive effects on domestic innovation.
Moser 2013 Moser Patents and Innovation: Evidence from Economic History Journal of Economic Perspectives (web) 10.1257/jep.27.1.23 Synthesis of the history: most important innovations were unpatented; patents shape direction more than rate.
BoldrinLevine 2013 Boldrin & Levine The Case against Patents Journal of Economic Perspectives (web) 10.1257/jep.27.1.3 Strong-form skeptic position: "no empirical evidence that [patents] serve to increase innovation and productivity."
Zhang 2024 Zhang, Zhu & Liu Patent length and innovation: Novel evidence from China Technological Forecasting & Social Change (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.techfore.2023.123010 Honest balance: a patent-length extension raised patent applications ~30% — patents can spur in some settings.
GalassoMitchell 2018 Galasso, Mitchell & Virag A theory of grand innovation prizes Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2017.11.009 Theory: patent races reward speed of discovery over quality.
Dechezlepretre 2023 Dechezlepretre, Einio, Martin, Nguyen & Van Reenen Do Tax Incentives Increase Firm Innovation? An RD Design AEJ: Economic Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20200739 Evidence that R&D tax incentives buy real innovation — a "push" alternative/complement to patents.
Hopenhayn 2006 Hopenhayn, Llobet & Mitchell Rewarding Sequential Innovators: Prizes, Patents, and Buyouts Journal of Political Economy (topecon) 10.1086/510562 Cleaner reward mechanisms for sequential innovation (prize-and-buyout menus).
Buyouts 2016 Galasso, Mitchell & Virag Market outcomes and dynamic patent buyouts Int'l J. of Industrial Organization (fieldindustrialorganization) 10.1016/j.ijindorg.2016.06.007 Dynamic patent-buyout schemes as an alternative reward design.
HopenhaynSquintani 2016 Hopenhayn & Squintani Patent Rights and Innovation Disclosure Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdv030 Objection: patents also serve a disclosure function (a benefit a pure subsidy/prize wouldn't deliver).
Gambardella 2023 Gambardella Private and social functions of patents Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2023.104806 Objection: patents grease markets for technology, signal value, and disclose — beyond the incentive function.
