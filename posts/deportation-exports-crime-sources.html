Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
Sviatschi 2022a Sviatschi, M. M. Spreading Gangs: Exporting US Criminal Capital to El Salvador AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20201540 Anchor. Deportation → gangs, gang-specific crime, homicide, child recruitment, return migration; municipality-of-birth instrument.
Kalsi 2018 Kalsi, P. The impact of US deportation of criminals on gang development and education in El Salvador J Dev Econ (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2018.08.010 Independent confirmation of the deportation→gang link; business-dense regions more violent; schooling falls (boys most).
Escobar et al. 2023 Escobar, Tobón & Vanegas-Arias Production and persistence of criminal skills J Dev Econ (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2022.102969 Mechanism: criminal skill produced/transmitted in prison, persists; "specialists" and "trainees."
Sviatschi 2022b Sviatschi, M. M. Making a Narco: Childhood Exposure to Illegal Labor Markets and Criminal Life Paths Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA17082 Mechanism: exposure builds industry-specific criminal capital; ~30% ↑ adult incarceration; schooling mitigates.
Lonsky 2025 Lonsky, J. Gulags, crime, and elite violence: Origins and consequences of the Russian mafia J Public Econ (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2025.105361 Convergent natural experiment: relocated criminals (Gulag) seed mafia; violence erupts on state collapse.
Acemoglu et al. 2020 Acemoglu, De Feo & De Luca Weak States: Causes and Consequences of the Sicilian Mafia REStud (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdz009 Weak-state soil: mafia spreads where state is absent; persistent long-run harms.
Abreha et al. 2025 Abreha, Johnson & Robertson Is crime a "root cause" of Central American emigration? Evidence from El Salvador J Dev Econ (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2025.103456 Boomerang: Bukele crackdown cut homicides → US border encounters with Salvadorans down 45–67%.
Bianchi et al. 2012 Bianchi, Buonanno & Pinotti Do Immigrants Cause Crime? JEEA (subtopecon) 10.1111/j.1542-4774.2012.01085.x Objection: immigration net effect on crime ≈ 0 (robberies only) → backfire needs a selected margin.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
Bayer et al. 2009 Bayer, Hjalmarsson & Pozen Building Criminal Capital Behind Bars QJE (topecon) 10.1162/qjec.2009.124.1.105 Criminal capital is taught: peer effects reinforce existing offense types.
Stevenson 2017 Stevenson, M. Breaking Bad: Social Influence and the Path to Criminality in Juvenile Jails REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/REST_a_00685 Social contagion of crime-oriented attitudes/norms.
Aizer & Doyle 2015 Aizer & Doyle Juvenile Incarceration, Human Capital, and Future Crime QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjv003 Institutions can breed crime: incarceration ↓ schooling, ↑ recidivism.
Damm & Dustmann 2014 Damm & Dustmann Does Growing Up in a High Crime Neighborhood Affect Youth Criminal Behavior? AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.104.6.1806 Exposure breeds crime even in a strong state (quasi-random refugee assignment).
Blattman et al. 2025 Blattman, Duncan, Lessing & Tobón Gang Rule: Understanding and Countering Criminal Governance REStud (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdae079 Gangs govern millions; fill power vacuums (and can complement the state).
Mirenda et al. 2022 Mirenda, Mocetti & Rizzica The Economic Effects of Mafia: Firm Level Evidence AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20201015 Mafia diffusion into northern areas "with no Mafia tradition."
Fenizia & Saggio 2024 Fenizia & Saggio Organized Crime and Economic Growth: Municipalities Infiltrated by the Mafia AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20221687 Mafia infiltration/removal; frames the origin-and-spread literature.
Orozco-Alemán & González-Lozano 2018 Orozco-Alemán & González-Lozano Drug Violence and Migration Flows: Lessons from the Mexican Drug War J Human Resources (fieldothers) 10.3368/jhr.53.3.0215-6948R4 Violence → migration (+1.53 pp); route-violence deters (nuance).
Leo et al. 2024 Leo, Jurado & Ramírez-Álvarez Internal migration and drug violence in Mexico J Dev Econ (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jdeveco.2024.103334 Violence drives migration; welfare cost ~10% of GDP/yr.
Ajzenman et al. 2023 Ajzenman, Domínguez & Undurraga Immigration, Crime, and Crime (Mis)Perceptions AEJ: Applied (subtopecon) 10.1257/app.20210156 Immigration → null on crime, only ↑ fear/defensive spending.
Mastrobuoni & Pinotti 2015 Mastrobuoni & Pinotti Legal Status and the Criminal Activity of Immigrants AEJ: Applied (subtopecon) 10.1257/app.20140039 Legal status → −50% recidivism: opportunity, not removal, cuts crime.
Freedman et al. 2018 Freedman, Owens & Bohn Immigration, Employment Opportunities, and Criminal Behavior AEJ: Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20150165 Closing off legal jobs → ↑ felony charges.
East et al. 2023 East, Hines, Luck, Mansour & Velásquez The Labor Market Effects of Immigration Enforcement J Labor Econ (fieldothers) 10.1086/721152 Enforcement (Secure Communities) cut US-born employment/wages too.
Yang 2008 Yang, D. Can enforcement backfire? Crime displacement … customs reform in the Philippines REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/rest.90.1.1 Enforcement displaces rather than eliminates; total unchanged.
Bellégo & Drouard 2024 Bellégo & Drouard Fighting Crime in Lawless Areas: Evidence from Slums in Rio de Janeiro AEJ: Policy (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20200587 Pacification cut murders/robberies but shifted criminals to assaults/threats.
