Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Sarsons 2021 Sarsons, Gërxhani, Reuben & Schram Gender Differences in Recognition for Group Work JPE (topecon) 10.1086/711401 The anchor. Tenure: +1 coauthored paper = +7.4pp men / +4.7pp women; +1 solo = +9.5pp men / +15.5pp women; penalty concentrated in coauthoring with men (+7.8pp him / +1.8pp her); no penalty in sociology; two experiments show biased attribution net of confounds; credit gap ≈40% of raw / ~65% of conditional tenure gap.
Card et al. 2022 Card, DellaVigna, Funk & Iriberri Gender Differences in Peer Recognition by Economists Econometrica (topecon) 10.3982/ECTA18027 The boundary / counter-evidence. Conditional on output, Econometric Society Fellowship gap pro-male pre-1980 (4×) → ~0 (1980–2009) → pro-female 2010–19 (2×); no Sarsons-style coauthor penalty in Fellow selection. Scopes the claim to marginal/ambiguous credit.
Babcock et al. 2017 Babcock, Recalde, Vesterlund & Weingart Gender Differences in Accepting and Receiving Requests for Tasks with Low Promotability AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20141734 Supply-side: women volunteer for low-credit tasks 48% more; asked more (39% vs 27%); gap vanishes in single-sex groups (beliefs, not preferences).
Coffman 2014 Coffman Evidence on Self-Stereotyping and the Contribution of Ideas QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qju023 Supply-side: in male-typed domains women withhold correct ideas ~14pp more at top ability (+93%); driven by confidence, not ability.
Bohnet et al. 2016 Bohnet, van Geen & Bazerman When Performance Trumps Gender Bias: Joint vs. Separate Evaluation Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2186 Demand-side + the fix. Separate eval: 65% pick lower-performing man, 44% higher-performing woman. Joint eval: 57% pick the higher-performing woman, ~3% the weaker man. Belief-based, not taste.
Exley & Kessler 2022 Exley & Kessler The Gender Gap in Self-Promotion QJE (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjac003 Supply-side: women rate own performance ~13 points lower (0–100, ≈24% of mean) than equally performing men; not strategic/risk; appears by 6th grade.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Vakili et al. 2022 Vakili, Teodoridis & Bikard Detrimental Collaborations in Creative Work: Evidence from Economics Organization Science (topman) 10.1287/orsc.2021.1501 Econ's alphabetical-ordering norm scrambles credit (front-of-alphabet names capture more), so author order carries no contribution signal — the ambiguity the bias feeds on.
Babcock et al. 2017 (backlash) Babcock, Recalde & Vesterlund Gender Differences in the Allocation of Low-Promotability Tasks: The Role of Backlash AER: P&P (topecon) 10.1257/aer.p20171018 Rules out backlash-for-saying-no as the driver of the volunteering gap.
Soc. recognition 2025 Banerjee & Mustafi (2025) Using Social Recognition to Address the Gender Difference in Volunteering for Low-Promotability Tasks Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2022.04017 A fix: public recognition closes much of the volunteering gap.

Background / seminal lineage

Heilman & Haynes 2005 Heilman & Haynes No Credit Where Credit Is Due: Attributional Rationalization of Women's Success in Male–Female Teams Journal of Applied Psychology (web) 10.1037/0021-9010.90.5.905 The seminal demand-side result: under ambiguity women rated less competent (5.6 vs 7.3), influential, leader-like; penalty vanishes when individual contribution is made unambiguous.
