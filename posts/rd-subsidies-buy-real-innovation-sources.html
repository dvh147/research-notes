Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI How used
Howell 2017 Howell Financing Innovation: Evidence from R&D Grants AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20150808 Cleanest grant RD: DOE SBIR Phase 1 ($150k) ~doubles VC (10->19%), +>=30% cite-wtd patents, +revenue; effects in constrained firms; funding not certification; Phase 2 null.
Santoleri 2024 Santoleri, Mina, Di Minin & Martelli The Causal Effects of R&D Grants (EU SME Instrument) REStat (subtopecon) 10.1162/rest_a_01233 Grant RD across 40+ countries: +15-31% patents, +24-33% employment, equity ~tripled, exit down; Seal-of-Excellence (no-money) firms do NOT improve = decisive funding-vs-certification test; entry spillovers; returns ~ SBIR.
Dechezlepretre 2023 Dechezlepretre, Einio, Martin, Nguyen & Van Reenen Do Tax Incentives Increase Firm Innovation? RD design AEJ:EP (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20200739 Tax-credit RD: GBP1.7 R&D per GBP1; R&D ~doubled, patents +60% (quality holds), 7-yr persistence; elasticity ~2.6; +10% aggregate UK R&D; positive spillovers, no business-stealing; constrained firms drive it.
Agrawal 2020 Agrawal, Rosell & Simcoe Tax Credits and Small Firm R&D Spending AEJ:EP (subtopecon) 10.1257/pol.20140467 Canada SR&ED: +17% total R&D among eligible small firms; larger for refund (cash-constrained) firms; contract R&D most elastic.
Bajgar 2025 Bajgar & Srholec Crowding in or crowding out? (Czech) J. Public Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2025.105357 Grant RD: SMEs crowd IN (1 unit subsidy -> ~2.3 units R&D), sustained; large firms crowd OUT; but no gain in patents/employment/productivity (the input->output caveat); constraints key.
Chen 2021 Chen, Liu, Suarez Serrato & Xu Notching R&D Investment with Corporate Income Tax Cuts in China AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20191758 The relabeling objection: 24.2% of reported R&D is relabeled (so ~76% real); doubling R&D -> +9% productivity; notch beats credit when relabeling rife; spillovers justify program.
Melnik-Smyth 2024 Melnik & Smyth R&D tax credits and innovation J. Public Economics (fieldothers) 10.1016/j.jpubeco.2024.105157 Soft-spot evidence: US state credits raise R&D spending but NO aggregate patenting increase, no citation-quality gain (novelty + market value do rise); driven by large firms.
David-Hall-Toole 2000 David, Hall & Toole Is public R&D a complement or substitute for private R&D? A review Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/S0048-7333(99)00087-6 Hook + historical baseline: 35 years of evidence "ambivalent", ~1/3 of studies found crowding out. The shrug the modern RD literature answers.

Supporting papers (cited for specific points)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI Point it supports
Criscuolo 2019 Criscuolo, Martin, Overman & Van Reenen Some Causal Effects of an Industrial Policy AER (topecon) 10.1257/aer.20160034 The constrained-firm law: +10% employment from subsidies, small firms only; large firms "accept subsidies without increasing activity"; no TFP gain.
Szucs 2020 Szucs Do research subsidies crowd out private R&D of large firms? (EU Framework) Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2020.103923 Large firms: on average no private-R&D increase; effect only for smaller / R&D-intensive firms; H2020 worked, predecessors didn't.
Marino 2016 Marino, Lhuillery, Parrotta & Sala Additionality or crowding-out? An overall evaluation Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2016.04.009 Crowding-out counter-evidence (France): no additionality or substitution; crowding-out worse at medium-high subsidy and under the tax-credit regime.
Dimos-Pugh 2016 Dimos & Pugh The effectiveness of R&D subsidies: a meta-regression analysis Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2016.01.002 Meta of 52 studies: reject full crowding-out, but average additionality "not substantial." The muted average.
Castellacci-Lie 2015 Castellacci & Lie Do the effects of R&D tax credits vary across industries? A meta-regression Research Policy (fieldinnov) 10.1016/j.respol.2015.01.010 Additionality systematically stronger for SMEs, services, low-tech, incremental schemes.
Akcigit 2021 Akcigit, Hanley & Serrano-Velarde Back to Basics: Basic Research Spillovers, Innovation Policy, and Growth Rev. Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdaa061 Design matters: uniform subsidies can worsen misallocation by over-rewarding applied research; basic-research support welfare-improving.
Islam 2018 Islam, Fremeth & Marcus Signaling by early stage startups: US government research grants and VC funding J. Business Venturing (fieldentrepreneurship) 10.1016/j.jbusvent.2017.10.001 The signaling channel exists (+12% VC for grant winners, larger for fewer-patent firms) - but the RD studies show funding, not certification, dominates.
BGV 2002 Bloom, Griffith & Van Reenen Do R&D tax credits work? Evidence from a panel of countries 1979-1997 J. Public Economics (web) 10.1016/S0047-2727(01)00086-X Older benchmark: 10% lower R&D cost -> ~+1% R&D short-run, ~+10% long-run (long-run ~unit-elastic, slow).
BVW 2019 Bloom, Van Reenen & Williams A Toolkit of Policies to Promote Innovation J. Economic Perspectives (web) 10.1257/jep.33.3.163 Placement: R&D tax credits + direct grants are among the most productive short-run tools; human capital more effective long-run.

Background / seminal lineage (web-verified)

Key Authors (year) Title Source DOI / URL Why included
Wallsten 2000 Wallsten The effects of government-industry R&D programs on private R&D: the case of the SBIR program RAND J. of Economics (web) 10.2307/2601032 The classic crowding-out foil to Howell on the SAME program (Phase II, IV design, R&D-dollar outcome): ~dollar-for-dollar crowding out, no employment effect.
Goolsbee 1998 Goolsbee Does Government R&D Policy Mainly Benefit Scientists and Engineers? AER P&P 88(2):298-302 (web) NBER w6532 The wage-bidding caveat: inelastic scientist supply -> subsidies raise wages; conventional estimates may be 30-50% too high.
BSV 2013 Bloom, Schankerman & Van Reenen Identifying Technology Spillovers and Product Market Rivalry Econometrica (web) 10.3982/ECTA9466 The spillover wedge that justifies the BOTE: social return to R&D ~55% vs private ~21%.
Lucking 2019 Lucking, Bloom & Van Reenen Have R&D Spillovers Changed? (Have R&D spillovers declined in the 21st century?) Fiscal Studies 40(4) (web) NBER w24622 Updates the social/private return ratio to ~4:1.
