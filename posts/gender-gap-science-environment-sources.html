Core papers (analyzed in depth)

Key Authors (year) Title Journal (tier) DOI / URL How used
Bell et al 2019 Bell, A.; Chetty, R.; Jaravel, X.; Petkova, N.; Van Reenen, J. Who Becomes an Inventor in America? The Importance of Exposure to Innovation Quarterly J. of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qjy028 Centerpiece: gender-specific childhood exposure; gaps persist controlling for early math (→ not ability); exposure would halve the innovation gender gap.
Breda et al 2023 Breda, T.; Grenet, J.; Monnet, M.; Van Effenterre, C. How Effective are Female Role Models in Steering Girls Towards STEM? Economic Journal (subtopecon) 10.1093/ej/uead019 RCT: a 1-hour role-model visit moved high-achieving girls into selective STEM.
Gallen 2023 Gallen, Y. Motherhood and the Gender Productivity Gap J. of the European Economic Assoc. (subtopecon) 10.1093/jeea/jvad064 Production-function estimate: ~8% productivity gap; motherhood central; pay–productivity decoupling for childless women in childbearing years.
Hoisl & Mariani 2017 Hoisl, K.; Mariani, M. It's a Man's Job: Income and the Gender Gap in Industrial Research Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2015.2357 Female inventors 4.2% of inventors, paid ~14% less — for equal-quality inventions. Gap in reward, not science.
Stuart & Galperin 2025 Stuart, H.C.; Galperin, R.V. The Quantity–Quality Tradeoff: How Incentives and Monitoring Shape Gender Differences at Work Organization Science (topman) 10.1287/orsc.2023.17754 USPTO examiners: org incentives/monitoring (not preferences) generate the measured gender quantity–quality gap.
Waldinger 2012 Waldinger, F. Peer Effects in Science: Evidence from the Dismissal of Scientists in Nazi Germany Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdr029 Honest calibration: no local-colleague peer effects → generic proximity is not the channel; the specific tie is.
Saccardo et al 2018 Saccardo, S.; Pietrasz, A.; Gneezy, U. On the Size of the Gender Difference in Competitiveness Management Science (topman) 10.1287/mnsc.2016.2673 Objection (preferences): most competitive 10% all men; only 1/5 of top quartile women.
Boudreau & Kaushik 2023 Boudreau, K.J.; Kaushik, N. Gender Differences in Responses to Competitive Organization? Organization Science (topman) 10.1287/orsc.2022.1624 Rebuttal: among STEM workers (n=97,678) there is no gender competitiveness gap.
Flory et al 2015 Flory, J.A.; Leibbrandt, A.; List, J.A. Do Competitive Workplaces Deter Female Workers? Review of Economic Studies (topecon) 10.1093/restud/rdu030 Rebuttal: competition aversion attenuates with teamwork, framing, age, outside options — context-sensitive.

Buser et al 2014 Buser, T.; Niederle, M.; Oosterbeek, H. Gender, Competitiveness, and Career Choices Quarterly J. of Economics (topecon) 10.1093/qje/qju009 Lab-measured competitiveness predicts academic-track choice; explains part of the gap.
